## Findings

- The dealer-hedging mechanism itself is academically established, not folklore: option-market-maker companies delta-rebalancing measurably affects underlying volatility [S2] and liquidity [S10], and drives expiration-day strike clustering in equities [S3, S4].
- BUT predictive power of *public, OI-derived* GEX is thin once you control for information you already have: SPY 2018–2026, raw GEX↔next-day realized-vol rank correlation −0.36 → −0.03 (p=0.18) after VIX + ATM IV controls; DEX "dead on arrival"; VEX mostly a vol proxy; CHEX weak/not robust [S9]. Independent replication of SqueezeMetrics' own method finds r²≈0.15 with next-day vol ex-GFC and NO relationship with signed returns; the white paper's headline result leans on the 2008 sample [S15].
- GEX numbers are not comparable across providers: formulas differ (dollar-gamma vs Taylor expansion with S² and a 0.5 factor) producing up to ~2,250× differences for the same chain; only relative within-platform strike rankings/trends are meaningful [S14].
- Dealer gamma sign assumption is the weakest link: public GEX assumes customers are always long calls / short puts. Quant StackExchange user implementing SqueezeMetrics GEX on NIFTY weekly options found implied "positive gamma" on ~95% of days simply because NIFTY call OI > put OI — i.e., in India the naive sign convention is close to a constant [S-stackoverflow via S14-adjacent search result, quant.stackexchange.com/questions/82436].
- Sign matters a lot when it's right, though: Dim–Eraker–Vilkov (with position-informed inventory estimation) find MM 0DTE inventory gamma is on average positive and inversely related to subsequent intraday vol; positive-gamma days show intraday REVERSAL, negative-gamma days MOMENTUM [S5]. Barbon–Buraschi confirm the sign×liquidity interaction: negative gamma imbalance + illiquidity → momentum, and gamma imbalance predicts flash-crash frequency/magnitude [S11].
- 0DTE does NOT destabilize the index on average: 0DTE-day index vol is 60–90 annualized bps LOWER, exploiting exogenous expiration-calendar variation [S7]; 0DTE OI gamma is associated with lower same-day realized vol and responses to 0DTE volume shocks are ~0.1σ — negligible [S25, and Cboe re-analysis]. The dissenting study finds +24.5% vol per 1σ of 0DTE volume [S26] — weight of evidence favors attenuation/stabilization.
- Structural test failure: a Princeton dissertation decomposing order flow into exogenous vs dealer-hedging flow finds the canonical gamma-feedback channel adds NO meaningful predictive power for intraday variance forecasts (QLIKE, Pearson, Spearman) — even where the 0DTE↔vol correlation exists, the gamma mechanism does not explain it [S8].
- 0DTE/expiry-day option BUYER economics are bad: average returns and Sharpe ratios of ATM delta-hedged calls/straddles are significantly negative; 0DTE variance risk premium ≈ 4–5× longer-dated (annualized), but in daily terms ~0.01% — unharvestable after costs; payout distribution has positive skewness ("lottery") [S5-summary, Almeida et al. "0DTE Asset Pricing" via fmai.memberclicks.net]. Consistent with SEBI: 91.5% of Indian retail options traders lose money vs ~60% in futures [S24].
- Vanna & charm: dealers are documented to be MORE sensitive to IV changes than spot moves; on 0DTE specifically, charm (delta-decay) showed a statistically significant edge restricted to the correct intraday window relative to expiration [S12]. Charm flows accumulate into the close on expiry days and are "small per minute but cumulatively significant"; vanna dominates quiet vol-compression tape, gamma dominates violent tape [S13].
- Pinning: the mechanism is real in US equities [S3, S4] but the popular "max pain" implementation fails out-of-sample: only 41.7% of 242 tracked expirations closed closer to max pain than the prior snapshot distance, and median distance widened (2.6%→3.4%) [S17]; Indian 17-expiry backtest ≈47% convergence [S18]; self-reported NIFTY band stats: within 25 pts ~20–25%, within 50 pts ~35–45% [S16]. "Max pain" predictability that does exist looks like generic short-term reversal, concentrated in small/illiquid stocks [S19].
- India-specific expiry effects: pre-weekly-options (pre-Feb-2019) NIFTY had clear expiry-day vol/price anomalies, which largely vanished after weeklies launched [S20]; longer-window studies still find elevated returns/volume/volatility on expiry day + the day after [S22]; moving the expiry day just MOVES the anomaly (BankNifty expiries: vol & volume rose on the new day) [S21]. Practical range data: NIFTY expiry-day range ~25–30% wider than average day (180–220 vs 140–170 pts), direction a coin flip, ~68% of days finish inside the open's ATM straddle, first 45 min + last 90 min ≈ 60% of range [S27].
- Post-Sept-2025 regime: all NSE index expiries now fall on TUESDAY [S23], so the NIFTY weekly "0DTE day" is Tuesday; expect theta-burn to concentrate over the Fri→Mon weekend and Monday positioning to matter more (sell-side commentary in ET coverage of the shift).
- Post-OpEx dynamic ("gamma unclench"): sell-side practitioners treat post-expiry sessions as vol-expansion windows because hedging barriers roll off [S28, SpotGamma summary of McElligott notes] — plausible and consistent with [S4]'s finite-pinning-probability model, but no peer-reviewed validation found.

## Folklore vs evidence

| Belief | Verdict | Source |
|--------|---------|--------|
| Positive dealer gamma suppresses realized vol; negative amplifies it | TRUE mechanistically, but mostly redundant with VIX/IV information | S2, S5, S11; redundancy: S9, S15 |
| GEX predicts market direction | FALSE — no relationship with signed returns | S9, S15, S1 |
| "Dealers are short gamma / long gamma" can be read off public OI with the call+/put− convention | WEAK/UNRELIABLE — sign assumption unverifiable; on NIFTY the naive convention yields positive GEX ~95% of days; providers disagree wildly | S14, QuantSE 82436, S12 (only proprietary flow data gives real dealer signs: S6) |
| 0DTE trading destabilizes the index / causes vol shocks | MOSTLY FALSE — average effect is vol attenuation (60–90 bps); one dissenting study | S7, S25 vs S26 |
| Gamma-flip level predicts acceleration | PLAUSIBLE but UNVALIDATED academically; the structural feedback test fails (S8); desk folklore | S8, S28, S11 (conditional on illiquidity) |
| Max Pain: NIFTY "magnetizes" to the max-pain strike on expiry | MOSTLY FALSE — ~41.7% US tracker, ~47% Indian sample (coin flips); residual signal is generic reversal | S17, S18, S19 |
| Pinning to high-OI strikes on expiry day is real | TRUE as a *continuous-value hedging* phenomenon (equities), strongest when MM net long gamma + large OI at a strike; NOT the same as max pain | S3, S4 |
| Buying morning-gamma ATM options on expiry day is a good trade | FALSE on average — systematically negative expected returns for long 0DTE/ATM strategies; positive skew is the lure | S5, Almeida et al., S24 |
| Expiry day is higher range but directionless | TRUE for NIFTY: +25–30% range, 52/48 direction split, ~68% inside open straddle | S27 |
| Vanna/charm flows add an edge beyond gamma | PARTIALLY TRUE on 0DTE within specific intraday windows (charm); vanna matters in vol-compression regimes | S12, S13 |

## Implications for our bot

Build it, but build a NARROW, honest version ranked by evidence strength:

1. **OI-concentration pin levels (DO — cheap, evidence-backed).** Compute per-strike OI-weighted gamma map from the NSE option chain each snapshot (BSM gamma formulae; NIFTY chain is free from NSE). Output to the LLM: top-2 call-OI and put-OI strikes (the traditional "walls"), plus the strike with max combined ±OI net gamma within ±1% of spot. Justification: strike clustering/pinning via delta-hedging is one of the most replicated findings in the options literature [S3, S4]. Framing to LLM: "support/resistance magnets," NOT "guaranteed pins."
2. **Net-GEX regime flag (DO, but as a low-weight tiebreaker).** Per-strike dollar gamma with the SpotGamma/SqueezeMetrics sign convention, flagged as positive/neutral/negative regime. Evidence says the raw regime↔next-day-vol link exists (−0.36 rank corr [S9]) but is subsumed by India VIX + ATM IV. So: only include GEX AFTER the bot already features India VIX level and ATM IV; expect it to add little standalone. Be aware the naive call+/put− convention is positive ~95% of NIFTY days — so treat the *sign* locally (per-strike around spot) rather than as a market-wide number.
3. **Max-pain level (DO with a warning label, or skip).** It is free to compute but measures "~coin-flip magnet": 41.7%–47% hit rate as a pull [S17, S18]; within-50-pts band ~35–40% [S16]. If included, present it to the LLM explicitly as "weak: better than random within wide bands only" and never as a directional target.
4. **Vanna/charm intraday note (DO as a soft contextual feature on Tuesday).** On expiry day, charm flows cumulate into the 12:00–15:30 window and pin-type behavior is strongest after ~12:00 [S12, S13, S16]. Cheap implementation: compute aggregate charm Γ-style map for the current weekly expiry only; tell the LLM "post-noon expiry-day drift tends toward high-OI strikes; reversion probability rises in the final 90 minutes."
5. **Expiry-day session-shape knowledge (DO, pure context).** Hardcode into prompt: Tuesday range is ~25–30% wider than average [S27], direction is a coin flip, first 45 min + last 90 min carry ~60% of range, ~2/3 of expiry sessions finish inside the opening ATM straddle → buying morning ATM options full-hold is negative expectancy; the defensible long-gamma setups are (a) NOT holding to expiry, (b) entering only on signal of range breakout, sized for the fat right tail, because per-occurrence mean is negative [S5, Almeida].
6. **DON'T build:** gamma-flip-level directional accelerator as a standalone signal (structural test S8 failed; desk-level folklore S28); DEX signal; any absolute cross-provider GEX comparisons [S14]; any signal that assumes observable dealer signs without trade-flow data [S6].

**Expected hit-rate honesty:** pinning-related features should be presented to users/LLM as ~55–65% probability statements at best (direction 52/48, max-pain bands 35–65% depending on width — real but not tradeable alone). GEX-regime adds ~zero on top of VIX+IV by the best out-of-sample evidence [S9]. The honest value of this feature set is *risk framing* (where chop/magnets live, where NOT to buy premium), not directional alpha.

## Open questions

- No India-specific academic study replicates Dim/Eraker/Vilkov-style MM-inventory estimation on NSE data — NIFTY dealer sign (who is actually short the weekly straddle OI) remains unverifiable from public data alone. NSE publishes FII/DII/prop/client category OI data that could partially close this; worth a dedicated data-ingestion check.
- Tuesday-expiry regime is young (since Sep 2025): does the +25–30% expiry-day range effect [S27, measured under Thursday] persist, shrink, or migrate to Monday (charm burn over the weekend)? Needs ~6–12 months of Tuesday data before trusting any baked-in number.
- Is NIFTY pinning detectable intraday at 1-min resolution, and does proximity-to-high-OI-strike predict reversion in-sample on NSE data (analogous to [S3] which is US monthly expiries and single stocks)? Requires historical chain + futures tick data we may not yet have.
- Charm/vanna implementation for NIFTY: what IV source (NSE disseminated IV vs computed from quotes) is stable enough intraday to make vanna maps non-noisy?
- Does post-expiry "gamma unclench" produce a measurable Wednesday vol expansion on NIFTY, analogous to the SPX OpEx folklore [S28]? Testable with historical realized-vol data once Tuesday-expiry sample matures.
