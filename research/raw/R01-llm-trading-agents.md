# R01: LLM Trading-Agent Architectures & Benchmarks — Evidence, Critiques, and Lessons

Research date: 2026-09-16. Scope: academic + industry evidence on LLM-based trading agents (TradingAgents, FinMem, FinAgent, FinRobot, InvestLM, AlphaAgents, FinCon, QuantAgent, etc.), with emphasis on (a) architectures that beat baselines, (b) reproducibility / look-ahead bias critiques, (c) LLM vs simple quant baselines, (d) single- vs multi-agent debate, (e) cost/latency.

## Findings

### (a) Architectures that beat baselines — as reported, with caveats

- **TradingAgents** (firm-style multi-agent: fundamental/sentiment/technical analysts + bull/bear researcher debate + trader + risk team) reports, on AAPL Jan 1–Mar 29 2024: CR 26.62%, ARR 30.5%, Sharpe 8.21, MDD 0.91% vs Buy&Hold CR −5.23%, SR −1.29 [S1]. Its own GitHub README warns: "Backtest results are not guaranteed to match any published figure... treat the framework as a research scaffold, not a strategy with a fixed, replicable return" [S1].
- **FinMem** (single-agent + layered memory + persona) reports TSLA CR 61.78% / SR 2.68 vs B&H −18.63% / −0.54; NFLX CR 36.45% / SR 2.02; AMZN CR 4.89% / SR 0.23; wins with Wilcoxon significance vs best competitor [S2]. Test window was ~6 months on 5 stocks [S6].
- **FinAgent** (multimodal: numbers + news + K-line chart images, tool-augmented, dual-level reflection) reports >36% average profit improvement over 9–12 baselines across 6 stock/crypto datasets; 92.27% return (84.39% relative improvement) on its best dataset [S3].
- **FinCon** (manager–analyst hierarchy + conceptual verbal reinforcement + within-episode CVaR alerts) beats LLM-based and DRL-based agents on 8 single-stock tasks with among the lowest MDD; ablations credit the risk-control component [S10]. Reported per-ticker Sharpe up to 2.37, portfolio Sharpe up to 3.27 [S7].
- **AlphaAgents** (fundamental/sentiment/valuation micro-agents + structured debate protocol) outperforms its 15-stock benchmark in the risk-neutral profile on cumulative return and rolling Sharpe; notably keeps full debate logs for human review and override [S5].
- **QuantAgent** (Indicator/Pattern/Trend/Risk agents, price-only zero-shot) beats random, linear-regression and XGBoost baselines on 1-hour and 4-hour bars across BTC, Nasdaq futures, commodities, equities [S16].
- The recurring pattern across all of the above: strong numbers on **short windows (3 months – 1.5 years), small universes (3–30 tickers), and naïve baselines (Buy&Hold, MACD, RSI rules)** [S1, S2, S3, S6, S10].

### (b) Reproducibility / contamination critiques — the counterweight evidence

- **FINSABER** re-backtested FinMem/FinAgent/FinCon-style strategies over **two decades and 100+ symbols** with disclosures and transaction costs: FinMem's reported MSFT CR of 23.26% fell to **−22.04%** (Sharpe 1.440 → **−1.247**); NFLX Sharpe 2.017 → **−0.478** [S6]. Under fair selection tasks, a trivial Volatility-Effect stock picker (Sharpe 0.703) beat the FinCon selection agent (0.389) and Momentum factor (0.384) [S6].
- **The Alpha Illusion** reproduces TradingAgents and QuantAgent for one year on 5 tickers with full frictions (commission + **token inference cost** + spread + market impact): TradingAgents portfolio Sharpe **0.43 → 0.22**, QuantAgent **−0.96 → −1.15**; both end below Buy&Hold. Audit found **35 of 40 system×friction-component cells unmodeled** across FinMem/TradingAgents/FinAgent/FinCon/QuantAgent — i.e. reported gross Sharpe is an upper bound on net Sharpe [S7].
- **Profit mirage / pretraining contamination**: backtested LLM returns "evaporate once the model's knowledge window ends"; LLMs trained on post-hoc market narratives effectively memorize outcomes (e.g., may know "NVDA surged 190% in 2023") [S8, S9]. Look-Ahead-Bench shows standard open models exhibit measurable alpha decay attributable to leakage, while point-in-time-trained models generalize better as they scale [S9].
- Only papers using strictly **post-knowledge-cutoff samples** (Lopez-Lira & Tang, news-headline signal) survive this critique: GPT-4 scored 93.3%/88.8% portfolio-day hit rates on initial reactions and retained drift predictability out-of-sample; but returns **declined as LLM adoption rose**, consistent with arbitrage decay rather than persistent alpha [S11].
- Survey benchmark for this literature: median backtest ≈ **1.3 years**; reported annualized returns cluster at 15–30%; few papers model transaction costs at all [S17].

### (c) Do LLMs beat simple quant baselines in controlled tests?

- Broad-universe controlled test says **no for timing**: regime analysis — FinAgent Sharpe 0.12 in bull / −0.38 in bear; FinMem −0.19 / −0.97; both "too cautious when risk is rewarded and too aggressive when it is punished"; ATR-Band and ARIMA delivered positive Sharpe in every regime [S6]. Larger LLMs did **not** reliably beat smaller ones, and both were consistently bettered by simpler models [S6].
- Controlled tests say **yes for signal extraction layered onto quant frameworks**: LLM-filtered cross-sectional momentum beats plain long-only momentum both in-sample and truly out-of-sample, robust to transaction costs, strongest for concentrated high-conviction portfolios [S12]; GPT-4 headline classification adds drift predictability beyond traditional sentiment scores [S11].
- Live, real-money evidence (the strongest ecological validity available): Alpha Arena S1 — 4 of 6 frontier models lost money over 17 days; Qwen3-Max +22.3%, DeepSeek +4.9%, GPT-5 ≈ −59%→$4,126 from $10k [S13]. Production-scale record (7.5M invocations, 3,505 funded vaults): fleet was **not profitable**, 41% round-trip win rate vs 50% matched retail benchmark; frontier models' decision quality was **statistically indistinguishable**, though choice stability differed sharply by model family; sizing was volatility-blind (median leverage 5.0× in every volatility sextile); 43.2% of positions saw ≥+300bps favorable excursion yet 49.3% of those closed negative (agents capture almost none of the upside they reach) [S14].

### (d) Single-agent vs multi-agent debate

- Evidence *for* multi-agent structure is mostly ablation-internal (e.g., FinCon's risk-control and AlphaAgents' debate help within their own setup) [S5, S10], and architecture research adds interpretability and audit trails [S1, S7].
- General-purpose controlled studies are skeptical: majority voting alone captures most of multi-agent-debate gains across 7 benchmarks (debate adds little) [S19]; homogeneous debate consumes **2.1–3.4× more tokens** for equal/lower accuracy with sycophancy-driven failure modes (up to 85.5% modal adoption, up to 70% correct→incorrect flips) [S18]; across 36 model×benchmark configurations, debate wins <20% of the time [S7].
- Deployment-cost argument: FinCon deliberately *reduces* peer-to-peer communication cost via manager-analyst hierarchy — an implicit admission that naïve debate doesn't pay [S10]; The Alpha Illusion requires "multi-agent disaggregation" (net-return delta per agent) before crediting collaboration [S7].

### (e) Cost / latency measurements

- FINSABER's cost accounting: one composite FinAgent backtest cost **$198.24** in API fees; FinMem **$31.79** (GPT-4o-mini); the full experimental program approached **~$700**; embedding generation cost excluded [S6].
- Multi-agent debate token inflation: 2.1–3.4× tokens per problem (up to 28,631 tokens/problem) vs self-correction [S18] — directly scales latency and cost in a polling loop.
- Token/inference costs materially changed reproduced net results for TradingAgents/QuantAgent [S7]; cost-decomposition frameworks now require LLM inference cost to be netted from PnL before calling anything "alpha" [S15].
- Latency: NeurIPS'25 formalizes the latency–quality tradeoff — smaller/compressed models act faster on stale-environment decisions; an optimal operating point exists [S23]. QuantAgent — the only "HFT-flavored" LLM framework — operates at **1–4 hour bars**, not true intraday HFT, and admits predictive precision drops on 1–15 minute candles [S16]. QFinZero notes LLM trading benchmarks essentially lack minute-level data support entirely [S22].

### Options-specific findings (directly adjacent to our use case)

- Raw option chains (thousands of contracts × strikes × expiries) exceed LLM context or cost limits; direct text-to-code over chains hallucinates invalid tickers and violates constraints — recommended fix is a DSL/intermediate representation (OQL) with deterministic validation/execution downstream [S21].
- Hybrid options-wheel architecture uses the LLM only as a **Bayesian-network model builder** (context interpretation + analogous-history selection) and lets the probabilistic model make the decision — explicitly because LLMs lack numerically rigorous, consistent reasoning [from search result, arXiv 2512.01123].
- Practitioner documentation of LLM Greeks failures: sign flipping, multiplier omission, strike-vs-spot confusion, fabricated magnitudes — fix via structured per-leg prompts + deterministic calculators, not bigger models [AI Fin Hub articles].

## Failure modes documented

- **Parametric look-ahead / pretraining contamination**: model has memorized historical outcomes; backtests measure memory, not prediction; returns collapse after the knowledge-cutoff [S8, S9, S11].
- **Narrow-window/narrow-universe selection**: median backtest 1.3yr, ≤10 tickers; FinMem's 5-stock results reversed sign on 100+ symbols (MSFT CR 23.26%→−22.04%) [S6, S17].
- **Friction blindness**: 35/40 friction cells unmodeled across five named systems; token cost, spread, impact and commission turn positive gross Sharpe into below-buy-and-hold net [S7].
- **Regime miscalibration**: LLM strategy timing is anti-correlated with regime needs — too conservative in bulls, too aggressive in bears (FinMem bear-market Sharpe −0.97) [S6].
- **Debate pathology**: sycophantic conformity (≤85.5%), contextual fragility (≤70% correct→incorrect flips), consensus collapse (≤32.3pp oracle gap); debate can *amplify* caution or error rather than correct it [S18, from FOMC study in search results].
- **Volatility-blind sizing & poor realization**: production agents held median 5× leverage in all vol regimes; 11% of the book held 62% of liquidations; they let +300bps excursions close negative nearly half the time [S14].
- **Gross-proxy optimization trap**: RL improving BTC market-state classification accuracy *reduced* cumulative trading returns — improving narrative/directional quality ≠ improving net PnL [S7 (citing Jang et al. 2025)].
- **Numerical/hallucination errors on options**: invalid tickers, sign flips on Greeks, fabricated magnitudes, strike/spot confusion, cross-expiry aggregation errors [S21; AI Fin Hub failure taxonomy].
- **Non-determinism**: TradingAgents' own repo warns results vary with backbone model, temperature, date range, data quality, sampling [S1].

## Implications for our NIFTY options advisory bot

**Do:**
- **Keep the LLM out of the numbers.** All Greeks, OI metrics, PCR, max-pain, IV-rank, ATR, VWAP deviations must be computed deterministically in Python and handed to the LLM as a compact snapshot — exactly our planned architecture. LLM-as-calculator failure modes are well documented [S21 + practitioner sources]. Every number the LLM quotes should be traceable to a computed field.
- **Structure the advice, don't free-form it.** Use a constrained output schema (instrument, strike, expiry, direction, entry band, stop, target, rationale, confidence) — analogous to OQL's DSL/validation approach and AlphaAgents' logged-debate-with-human-override pattern [S21, S5]. Deterministic validation should reject malformed or constraint-violating advice before the human sees it.
- **Prefer single-agent + tool-augmented snapshot over naïve multi-agent debate.** The strongest replicated pattern for credible results is LLM-as-signal-interpreter feeding a quant framework (labeled exemplars: Lopez-Lira [S11], LLM-momentum [S12]). Debate adds 2–3× tokens/latency with no robust accuracy gain [S18, S19]. If we want a "bear case" second opinion, a single adversarial prompt in one call is cheaper and empirically adequate.
- **Design evaluation around the knowledge-cutoff.** Backtests earlier than the model's training cutoff are meaningless for us (parametric look-ahead) [S8, S9]. Evaluate advisory quality only on rolling forward windows: log advice timestamped at snapshot time, grade it on realized outcomes. Since a human executes, we can't run true backtests anyway — build the shadow/paper log from day one.
- **Manage the regime problem explicitly in code.** Documented failure: LLM timing is conservative in bulls/aggressive in bears [S6]. Encode regime detection (trend filter, VIX/vol regime) deterministically and gate/cap LLM advice by regime—don't ask the LLM to discover it.
- **Cost/latency budget is favorable for advisory, not autonomy.** At poll intervals of ~1–5 min with a compact snapshot (a few thousand tokens), API cost is trivial (< FINSABER's $31.79 for a full FinMem backtest [S6]) and 5–30s LLM latency is tolerable for human-executed scalps — but only because a human executes slowly anyway. Never pitch LLM latency as execution-grade (an LLM loop is slower than a 1-min candle; QuantAgent found 1–15min precision poor [S16]).
- **Use the favorable-excursion finding:** production agents captured almost none of +300bps MFE; a mechanical bracket recovered +39bps/position [S14]. Our deterministic layer should auto-suggest bracket SL/target from ATR at advice time rather than relying on the LLM's exit judgment.

**Don't:**
- **Don't let the LLM pick raw strikes from a full chain dump.** Chain is thousands of rows — context overflow + hallucination [S21]. Pre-filter (e.g., ATM±5, liquid expiries, OI>L threshold) deterministically before the LLM sees it.
- **Don't claim backtested alpha for the LLM layer.** Any validation we run must be post-cutoff, net of costs, and reported with the FINSABER-style disclaimers [S6, S7]. Headline "Sharpe 8" numbers from TradingAgents-style papers are not deployment evidence [S7].
- **Don't trust LLM confidence as a probability** — "language confidence is not tradable probability" [S7]. If we show confidence, calibrate it empirically from the advice log over time.
- **Don't build homogenous-agent consensus (e.g., same model playing bull + bear + risk manager) expecting hallucination filtering** — conformity dominates; use deterministic risk checks instead [S18, S19].
- **Don't expect intraday edge from price-pattern prompting at scalping timescales** — the best "HFT" LLM framework targets 1–4h bars and fails below 15 min [S16]; our value-add is combining computed microstructure/OI signals with judgment, speed-of-judgment, and a human's execution discretion.

## Open questions

- No published evidence exists on LLM agents trading index **options (nonlinear payoff, theta decay, IV crush)** specifically — all major frameworks (FinMem/FinCon/TradingAgents/FinAgent) are equity/crypto spot at daily+ frequency; QFinZero states options + minute-level support is absent from benchmarks [S22]. Our failure modes will be first-principles engineering, not literature-guided.
- Does an LLM advisory layer measurably improve a *human* scalper's expectancy (advice acceptance rate, win-rate delta vs unaided trades)? Requires our own logged trial — no literature precedent.
- Optimal snapshot cadence for NIFTY intraday given LLM latency (poll every 1/3/5 min?) — unexplored; depends on staleness sensitivity of our metrics.
- Model choice for Indian-market nuances (FII/DII flow language, NSE-specific chain conventions): whether finance-tuned open models (InvestLM-style [S20]) beat frontier general models on regime commentary is untested.
- Whether counterfactual-perturbation techniques (FactFin [S8]) or point-in-time models [S9] matter for us at all, given we only need forward inference, not backtests — likely no, but worth confirming the model doesn't leak NIFTY's *recurring* patterns (expiry-day pinning behavior is heavily documented online and could be memorized folklore rather than current structure).
