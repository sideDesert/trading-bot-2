# R02: LLM market-prediction accuracy & failure modes — can an LLM turn structured market snapshots into tradeable advice?

Date compiled: 2026-09-16. Scope: ChatGPT/GPT-4 sentiment-to-return studies + replications/critiques; LLM numeric/tabular failure modes; backtest-contamination (look-ahead) critiques; hybrid code-computes/LLM-interprets architectures; whether reasoning models (o-series, DeepSeek-R1, etc.) fix numeric market reasoning.

## Findings

**Sentiment → return prediction (topic a):**
- GPT-4 headline classification predicts next-day returns; strongest revised result: ~90% hitting rate on the *initial, non-tradable* reaction to post-cutoff news, plus significant 1–2 day drift, concentrated in small caps and negative news [S1].
- The tradable version is thin: long-short daily-rebalanced strategy ≈ 34 bps/day **before transaction costs**, authors state exploitation is feasible only for "market participants whose transaction costs are sufficiently low, such as market makers" [S1].
- Capability is emergent: GPT-1/GPT-2/BERT could not forecast returns at all; predictability scales with model capability, and decays as LLM adoption rises [S1].
- Aggregate-market version: ChatGPT good-news ratio on WSJ front pages predicts monthly-annual market returns, OOS R² ≈ 1.17% (Jan 2006–Dec 2022) — statistically significant but economically tiny; DeepSeek and other LLMs underperformed ChatGPT on the identical task [S13].
- Asymmetry: negative news correlates with contemporaneous returns but has **no predictive power** in both firm-level and market-level studies [S1][S13].

**Backtest contamination / look-ahead critiques (topic c):**
- A "Lookahead Propensity" recall probe of Llama-3.3-70B is materially positive throughout the in-sample period and collapses essentially to zero in the year right after the Dec-2023 cutoff; the LLM's predictive power is amplified exactly on high-LAP (memorized) firm-days and loses significance post-cutoff [S3].
- Quantified magnitude: attenuating memorized outcomes corrects in-sample strategy returns by up to **−67.1%** across five 7–14B models on mega-caps; out-of-sample (2025) returns move by <$8K / Sharpe ±0.10 — i.e., in-sample performance was mostly memory [S5].
- Look-Ahead-Bench shows standard LLMs (Llama-3.1, DeepSeek-3.2) exhibit significant alpha decay across the cutoff, while purpose-built Point-in-Time-trained models generalize better as they scale [S4].
- Subtlety: in-sample, Glasserman–Lin find *anonymized* headlines **outperform** named ones — the dominant in-sample bias is "distraction" (stock-specific general knowledge), stronger for large caps, not just return memorization; anonymization also helps genuine OOS deployment [S2].

**Numeric / tabular reasoning failure modes (topic b):**
- FAITH (S&P 500 10-K tables, masked numeric span recovery): top proprietary models sit at 89–96% accuracy (Claude-Sonnet-4 95.6%, Gemini-2.5-Pro 91.9%, GPT-4.1 89.2%), but a **4–8% numeric error rate persists at the top tier**; multivariate calculation drops even the best models 10–20 pp (Gemini-2.5-Pro 90.0%, Claude-Sonnet-4 80.0%) and drives mid-tier models to ~0% [S6].
- Open-weight small models hallucinate numbers at 50–70% rates on financial tables (Llama-3.1-8B 47.5% accuracy, Qwen-3-8B 30.6%) [S6].
- V-FiLLM (answers correct by construction): error grows up to **51% with computation depth** and up to **47 pp under adversarial numeric perturbation**; verified-CoT LoRA only recovers 81.1%→85.6% [S12].
- FININDICES (full-length statements, up to 32K tokens): taking away formula hints collapses Gemini-3.1-Pro 70.70%→38.22%; single-value→multi-table synthesis costs ~10 pp even with hints [S18].
- Numeric-format sensitivity: identical arithmetic swings wildly with how digits are tokenized/formatted (R2L comma-grouped ≫ default); errors are systematic, not random noise [S9]. Frontier models still fail elementary magnitude comparisons and large-integer operations in 2026 [S10].
- Irrelevant numbers in context are the killer perturbation: open models lose ≥10% (max 51.55%) from injected numeric-only sentences vs stable performance on numeric-free distractors [S11].
- From price data specifically: ChatGPT-4o **over-extrapolates recent returns** expecting momentum when realized short-horizon returns show reversal; forecasting is biased optimistic and resistant to prompt engineering — a model built from human text inherits human behavioral biases [S7].

**Hybrid designs — code computes, LLM interprets (topic d):**
- FinRobot explicitly separates "deterministic financial computation" (pure-Python operators with full provenance) from LLM narration — this is a production-grade industry pattern, not just a lab result [S15].
- FINCON (NeurIPS 2024) assigns each analyst **one** data modality and keeps risk control (CVaR monitoring, exposure caps) as an external component the manager agent consults — risk math is never delegated to free-text reasoning [S17].
- Kim–Muhn–Nikolaev show the positive hybrid upside: anonymized, *standardized* numeric inputs + CoT that mimics an analyst's methodical workflow lift GPT-4 from 52% to **60%** directional accuracy (above analysts' 53–57%) — structure and prompting discipline are what unlock numeric-input value; the gain does not come from training memory [S8].

**Do reasoning models fix numeric market reasoning? (topic e)**
- Partially, generically: DeepSeek-R1 ≈ o1-1217 on math/code benchmarks [S14 refs], and FAITH's top scorers are reasoning-class models [S6].
- But finance-specific degradation persists: FinReason (29 LLMs) finds general reasoning models (GPT-o1, DeepSeek-R1, GPT-4.5) "exhibit obvious performance degradation in financial contexts," worse on long documents; an 8B domain-tuned model (Fin-o1) beats them [S14].
- On the WSJ market-prediction task, DeepSeek *underperformed* ChatGPT despite strong math reasoning — English corpus depth, not reasoning RL, drove the result [S13].
- Residual numeric fragility remains even in 2026 frontier models (basic magnitude/addition failures surveyed across "frontier models" in [S10]; numeric-perturbation sensitivity documented for "the most advanced commercial" models in [S11]).

## Failure modes documented

1. **Parametric look-ahead bias** — model recalls realized outcomes for events inside its training window; in-sample backtests inflated up to ~67%; vanishes at cutoff (the "smoking gun" collapse of recall to ~0) [S3][S4][S5].
2. **Distraction effect** — pre-trained knowledge about named companies contaminates sentiment scoring; anonymized headlines score *better* in-sample, especially for large caps [S2].
3. **Intrinsic numeric hallucination from tables** — wrong value recovery/calculation from in-context financial tables at 4–8% (frontier) up to 50–70% (small open) rates; worsens steeply with calculation depth and multivariate (2D-table) structure [S6][S12][S18].
4. **Tokenization/format-induced arithmetic errors** — same numbers, different decimal grouping/order, different answers; systematic error patterns indicate fragile internal arithmetic rather than estimation [S9][S10].
5. **Numeric-distractor sensitivity** — extraneous numbers in the prompt degrade reasoning up to ~51% [S11].
6. **Momentum over-extrapolation & optimism from price series** — LLM reads recent gains and forecasts more gains, opposite to short-horizon reversal in the data; prompt engineering barely fixes it [S7].
7. **Directional asymmetry of sentiment signal** — negative news predicts direction/continuation only contemporaneously, not forward; signal lives in *positive-news underreaction* [S1][S13].
8. **Thin edge vs. costs** — the best-documented text signal is ~34 bps/day pre-cost on daily-rebalanced L-S; a scalping advisory's transaction costs can silently eat the entire documented effect [S1].
9. **Fake-news / adversarial-input vulnerability** — live-benchmark LLMs degrade when fabricated news is mixed with indicators [S16].
10. **Context-length structural bottleneck** — performance drops when several tables/statements must be synthesized vs. one lookup; long snapshots are not free [S6][S18][S14].

## Implications for our NIFTY options advisory bot

**DO:**
- **Pipeline computes every number.** LTP changes, VWAP deviation, OI change %, PCR, Greeks, IV rank, candle-pattern stats, position P&L — Python only. The LLM never multiplies, divides, or aggregates [S6][S9][S10][S12][S15].
- **Give the LLM a compact, pre-digested snapshot**: pre-computed metrics rounded consistently (same decimals everywhere — formatting changes model behavior [S9]), plus short textual labels ("option chain shows aggressive call writing at 25,000"). Keep context small; every extra table row costs accuracy [S6][S18].
- **Use the LLM for interpretation & classification, not forecasting**: "does this pattern look like trend-continuation or exhaustion; what are the risks in this setup" — analogous to the tasks where LLMs demonstrably beat baselines (sentiment classification [S1], directional judgment with CoT [S8]).
- **Force structured CoT**: the 52%→60% lift in [S8] came from a prompt that mimics analyst workflow — our snapshot prompt should have a fixed reasoning scaffold, not free-form "should I buy?"
- **Ask for risks/uncertainty, not point targets**: LLM 80% interval forecasts are better calibrated than humans' [S7]; use it as a devil's advocate / checklist engine.
- **Log and diff numeric claims**: any number the LLM writes back to the human should be string-matched against pipeline-computed values; flag unsourced numbers (numeric provenance, à la FinRobot [S15]).
- **Only backtest on post-training-cutoff data**, and treat any in-window backtest number as an upper bound inflated by up to ~2/3 [S3][S5].

**DON'T:**
- **Don't feed raw price/OHLC series and ask "which way next?"** — documented momentum-over-extrapolation bias [S7] is exactly wrong for intraday reversal-heavy scalping.
- **Don't let the LLM recompute metrics, do position-size math, or combine multiple tables** — error rates of 10–50%+ on multi-step tabular computation [S6][S12][S18].
- **Don't pad the snapshot with irrelevant stats** — extraneous numbers measurably degrade output (−10% to −51%) [S11].
- **Don't trust in-training-window backtests or anecdotal "ChatGPT predicted the 2023 rally" demos** [S3][S4][S5].
- **Don't assume the newest reasoning model removes the problem** — domain degradation persists for o1/R1-class models [S14]; small/foot-in-the-door models are far worse [S6].
- **Don't automate execution on LLM output** — positive results in this literature are direction-consistent advice to a human (or heavily scaffolded multi-agent systems), not raw signals; keep the human-in-the-loop design.
- **Don't inject news/social text without filtering** — live benchmarks show fake news degrades prediction [S16]; if we add news, dedupe/source-verify first.

## Open questions

1. No peer-reviewed study tests LLM snapshot-reading on **Indian index options intraday** — all evidence is US equities, daily-to-annual horizons. Does the sentiment advantage survive 5-minute horizons and option convexity?
2. Does the Glasserman–Lin anonymization trick [S2] help us? (Probably N/A — NIFTY is a single index, but we could anonymize dates to prevent the model "remembering" specific historic days.)
3. Is the post-cutoff backtest window long enough for stat-sig intraday evaluation given options P&L variance?
4. Do per-model hallucination rates on *our specific snapshot schema* track FAITH's published rates? (We should run a masked-value self-test like [S6] on our own snapshots.)
5. Does giving the LLM tool-access (calculator) instead of pre-computed values outperform the static-snapshot design? Hybrid-literature suggests pre-computed + narration [S15], but no direct A/B at intraday timescales.
6. Can LLM risk-checking (veto bad setups) be validated quantitatively the way generation can?
